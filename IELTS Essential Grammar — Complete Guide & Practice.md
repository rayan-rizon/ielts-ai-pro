# IELTS Essential Grammar — Complete Guide & Practice Workbook

## How grammar affects the IELTS score

Grammar is assessed directly in **Writing** and **Speaking** under _Grammatical Range and Accuracy_. This criterion contributes one quarter of the score in each skill. Grammar also supports Reading and Listening because learners must recognise sentence relationships, word forms and the grammar required by gap-fill questions.

Official descriptors reward two separate abilities:

- **Range:** using an appropriate variety of simple, compound and complex structures.
- **Accuracy:** controlling grammar and punctuation so errors are infrequent and meaning remains clear.

| Target | Typical grammar performance                                                                                        | Main training priority                                                      |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Band 5 | Basic forms are more accurate; complex structures are limited and often faulty.                                    | Repair sentence boundaries, tense control, agreement, articles and plurals. |
| Band 6 | A mix of simple and complex forms is used, but complex grammar has frequent errors.                                | Use a controlled range of complex sentences without losing clarity.         |
| Band 7 | A variety of complex structures is used; error-free sentences are frequent; remaining errors do not block meaning. | Increase flexibility and eliminate repeated, systematic errors.             |
| Band 8 | A wide range is used flexibly; most sentences are error-free; errors are occasional rather than systematic.        | Refine precision, punctuation and natural structure choice.                 |

<aside>
⚠️

**Important:** “Complex” does not mean “long.” A clear sentence with one well-controlled dependent clause is better than a 45-word sentence containing several errors. Never force advanced grammar where a simple sentence communicates the idea more precisely.

</aside>

# Part I — The sentence system

## Unit 1: Complete sentences and clauses

A complete sentence normally needs a **subject**, a **finite verb** and a complete idea.

- Complete: _Public transport reduces congestion._
- Fragment: _Because public transport reduces congestion._
- Repaired: _Cities should invest in rail systems because public transport reduces congestion._

A **clause** contains a subject and a verb.

- **Independent clause:** can stand alone — _The policy failed._
- **Dependent clause:** cannot stand alone — _although the policy was expensive_

### Four useful sentence forms

1. **Simple:** _Remote work saves commuting time._
2. **Compound:** _Remote work saves time, but it may reduce social contact._
3. **Complex:** _Although remote work saves time, it may reduce social contact._
4. **Compound-complex:** _Although remote work saves time, some employees feel isolated, and employers must provide support._

### Avoid fragments

Dependent-clause starters such as _because, although, while, if, when_ cannot usually stand alone.

- Incorrect: _Because many graduates lack experience._
- Correct: _Many graduates struggle to find work because they lack experience._

### Avoid run-on sentences

Two complete sentences cannot be joined only by a comma.

- Incorrect: _The city expanded rapidly, housing became unaffordable._
- Correct: _The city expanded rapidly, and housing became unaffordable._
- Correct: _The city expanded rapidly. Consequently, housing became unaffordable._

### Practice 1

4. Although the scheme was costly, it reduced traffic, and residents supported its continuation.
5. The museum, which opened in 2018, attracts international visitors.

## Unit 2: Subject–verb agreement

A singular subject normally takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb.

- _The problem is serious._
- _The problems are serious._

### High-frequency IELTS traps

- **The number of + plural noun + singular verb:** _The number of cars has increased._
- **A number of + plural noun + plural verb:** _A number of cities have introduced charges._
- **Each/every/everyone/someone + singular verb:** _Each participant receives a certificate._
- **Uncountable noun + singular verb:** _Research shows that sleep matters._
- **Long subject:** _The rising cost of essential goods affects low-income families._
- **There is/are:** _There are several causes._ / _There is one major cause._

### Practice 2

2. A number of researchers **argues / argue** that the policy is ineffective.
3. Every child **need / needs** access to education.
4. There **is / are** several reasons for this trend.
5. Traffic congestion and air pollution **is / are** closely connected.

## Unit 3: Nouns, countability and quantity

### Countable nouns

They can be singular or plural: _a student, two students, many students, fewer students_.

### Uncountable nouns

They are not normally plural and do not use _a/an_: _advice, equipment, evidence, information, knowledge, research, traffic, pollution, work_.

- Incorrect: _many informations, an advice, fewer pollution_
- Correct: _much information, a piece of advice, less pollution_

### Quantity words

| Countable plural                         | Uncountable                        | Both                                         |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| many, a few, fewer, several, a number of | much, a little, less, an amount of | some, any, a lot of, plenty of, enough, more |

### Common irregular plurals

_child → children; person → people; phenomenon → phenomena; criterion → criteria; analysis → analyses._

2. There were less accidents after the speed limit changed.
3. The researchers collected many evidences.

## Unit 4: Articles — a, an, the and zero article

Use **a/an** for one non-specific singular countable noun.

- _A government can use taxation to change behaviour._
- _An effective policy requires public support._

Use **the** when the reader knows which person or thing you mean, when it has already been mentioned, or when it is unique in context.

- _A policy was introduced in 2020. The policy reduced waste._
- _The government should protect vulnerable citizens._

Use **no article** for general plural and uncountable ideas.

- _Governments regulate markets._
- _Education improves social mobility._
- _Technology changes how people work._

### Frequent errors

- Incorrect: _The technology has transformed society._
- Correct general statement: _Technology has transformed society._
- Incorrect: _Government should act._
- Usually correct in an essay about a country: _The government should act._
- Incorrect: _Students need an access to information._
- Correct: _Students need access to information._

### Practice 4

2. **[blank]** policy described in the report was introduced by **[blank]** local government.

4. A university should provide **[blank]** support for students with disabilities.

## Unit 5: Pronouns and reference

Pronouns must refer clearly to a noun and agree with it.

- Unclear: _When the council spoke to the company, they rejected the proposal._
- Clear: _The company rejected the council’s proposal._

Use **it** for a singular thing, **they/them** for plural nouns or a person whose gender is unknown, **this/these** to refer to a specific idea.

- Weak: _This is harmful._
- Better: _This loss of privacy is harmful._

### Relative pronouns

- **who** for people: _Students who work part-time need flexible schedules._
- **which** for things: _The policy, which began in May, reduced waste._
- **that** for defining people or things: _Measures that reduce emissions should be prioritised._
- **where** for places: _Cities where housing is scarce face rising rents._
- **whose** for possession: _Workers whose jobs are automated need retraining._

### Practice 5

1. Students which study abroad often become more independent.
2. The government changed the law, which made them unpopular.
3. Companies should support employees that jobs are affected by automation.
4. I visited a city which public transport is free.

# Part II — Verbs and time

## Unit 6: Essential tense control

Choose tense by **meaning and time**, not by trying to sound advanced.

| Tense              | Main IELTS use                                  | Example                                        |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Present simple     | general facts, opinions, routines               | _Public transport reduces emissions._          |
| Present continuous | temporary or developing situations              | _More companies are adopting flexible work._   |
| Present perfect    | past change connected to now; unfinished period | _Online learning has expanded since 2020._     |
| Past simple        | finished past time                              | _Sales fell in 2019._                          |
| Past perfect       | earlier of two past events                      | _By 2015, the city had completed the railway._ |
| Future forms       | prediction, plan or expected result             | _Demand will probably rise._                   |

### Since and for

- **since + starting point:** _since 2018_
- **for + duration:** _for eight years_
- Correct: _Car ownership has increased since 2018._

### Task 1 tense rule

Check the chart’s time period.

- Finished period: _Between 2000 and 2010, sales rose._
- Up to the present: _Since 2010, sales have risen._
- Forecast: _Sales are projected to rise by 2030._

### Keep parallel tense

- Incorrect: _Sales rose, reached a peak and then are declining._
- Correct: _Sales rose, reached a peak and then declined._

### Practice 6

Choose the best form.

1. Since 2015, the city **built / has built** three new hospitals.
2. The graph shows that unemployment **fell / has fallen** between 2010 and 2018.
3. By the time the ban began, many firms **changed / had changed** their packaging.
4. At present, more employees **work / are working** from home than a decade ago.
5. Experts predict that demand **rises / will rise** over the next five years.

## Unit 7: Verb patterns — gerunds and infinitives

Some verbs are followed by **-ing**, some by **to + verb**, and some by the bare infinitive.

### Common patterns

- **verb + -ing:** avoid, consider, enjoy, finish, suggest — _Governments should consider increasing the tax._
- **verb + to-infinitive:** aim, decide, hope, need, plan, want — _The scheme aims to reduce waste._
- **modal + base verb:** can improve, should invest, must regulate — not _must to regulate_
- **preposition + -ing:** _focus on improving; succeed in reducing; by investing_
- **allow/encourage + object + to-infinitive:** _The policy encourages residents to recycle._

### Meaning changes

- _stop smoking_ = end the habit
- _stop to smoke_ = pause another activity in order to smoke

### Practice 7

Correct the verb pattern.

1. The policy aims reducing traffic.
2. Governments should consider to subsidise public transport.
3. Technology allows people working remotely.
4. The authorities must to protect personal data.
5. Cities can reduce pollution by invest in railways.

## Unit 8: Modal verbs and strength of claims

Modals express possibility, ability, advice, obligation and prediction.

- **may/might/could:** possibility — _The policy may reduce congestion._
- **can:** ability or general possibility — _Remote work can improve flexibility._
- **should/ought to:** recommendation — _Schools should teach financial literacy._
- **must/have to:** strong obligation — _Drivers must obey the law._
- **would:** hypothetical result — _Lower fares would encourage bus use._

### Academic caution

Avoid unsupported certainty. Compare:

- Too absolute: _Technology will eliminate unemployment._
- Better: _Automation may displace some workers, particularly in routine occupations._

### Practice 8

Choose the most appropriate modal.

1. To protect patients, hospitals **must / might** maintain accurate records.
2. A lower tax **would / must** encourage investment if other conditions remained stable.
3. Excessive screen time **may / has to** affect sleep quality.
4. Governments **should / can** consider targeted support for low-income families. _(recommendation)_

# Part III — Building grammatical range

## Unit 9: Coordination and parallel structure

Use **and, but, or, so, yet** to join equal structures.

- _The policy is affordable and effective._
- _The policy reduced waste, but it increased costs._

Keep list items parallel.

- Incorrect: _The programme aims to reduce waste, improving health and the creation of jobs._
- Correct: _The programme aims to reduce waste, improve health and create jobs._

Use paired structures accurately:

- _both affordable and reliable_
- _either regulate the market or provide subsidies_
- _not only reduced congestion but also improved air quality_

### Practice 9

Correct the parallelism.

1. Public transport is cheaper, safer and it protects the environment.
2. The scheme aims to create jobs, reducing poverty and improve health.
3. The policy not only reduced waste but also household costs fell.

## Unit 10: Subordination — reason, contrast, condition and time

Subordination shows the logical relationship between ideas.

| Function            | Useful words                          | Example                                                        |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reason              | because, since, as                    | _Cycling increased because new lanes were built._              |
| Contrast/concession | although, even though, whereas, while | _Although the scheme is costly, its benefits are substantial._ |
| Condition           | if, unless, provided that             | _The policy will work if it is properly enforced._             |
| Time                | when, while, before, after, once      | _Demand fell after prices increased._                          |
| Purpose             | so that, in order to                  | _Taxes can be raised so that services can be improved._        |

### Double-conjunction errors

- Incorrect: _Although it is expensive, but it is effective._
- Correct: _Although it is expensive, it is effective._
- Correct: _It is expensive, but it is effective._

### Punctuation

Use a comma after an opening dependent clause.

- _Although rents rose, demand remained high._

A final dependent clause often needs no comma.

- _Demand remained high although rents rose._

### Practice 10

Combine each pair with the word given.

1. The train is expensive. It is faster than the bus. (**although**)
2. Cities invest in reliable transport. Car use will decline. (**if**)
3. Many people move to cities. Employment is more widely available there. (**because**)
4. Older people prefer traditional media. Younger adults rely on social platforms. (**whereas**)

## Unit 11: Relative clauses

A **defining** relative clause identifies the noun and uses no comma.

- _Students who receive feedback improve faster._

A **non-defining** clause adds extra information and uses commas.

- _The new library, which opened in June, is already popular._

Do not use **that** in a non-defining clause.

- Correct: _Dhaka, which is densely populated, faces severe congestion._

Reduced relative clauses create concise academic writing.

- Full: _Policies that are designed to reduce waste can change behaviour._
- Reduced: _Policies designed to reduce waste can change behaviour._

### Practice 11

Join the sentences with a relative clause.

1. The city introduced a congestion charge. The city has severe air pollution.
2. Workers need retraining. Their jobs are vulnerable to automation.
3. The programme supports families. These families earn below the median income.

## Unit 12: Conditionals

| Type   | Meaning                     | Form and example                                                     |
| ------ | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Zero   | general truth               | _If prices rise, demand usually falls._                              |
| First  | real future possibility     | _If the city improves buses, car use will decline._                  |
| Second | hypothetical present/future | _If fares were lower, more people would use trains._                 |
| Third  | imagined past               | _If the law had been enforced, the accident rate would have fallen._ |

Do not normally use **will** in the _if_-clause.

- Incorrect: _If governments will invest, pollution will fall._
- Correct: _If governments invest, pollution will fall._

Use mixed conditionals only when the time relationship is clear.

- _If the city had planned better, commuters would not face these delays today._

### Practice 12

Complete the sentences.

1. If public transport becomes more reliable, commuters \_\_\_ (use) it more often.
2. If I were the mayor, I \_\_\_ (prioritise) affordable housing.
3. If the company had trained its staff, fewer errors \_\_\_ (occur).
4. Unless emissions decline, air quality \_\_\_ (continue) to worsen.

## Unit 13: Comparisons

Use:

- _higher/lower than_
- _as high as / not as high as_
- _twice/three times as high as_
- _the highest/lowest proportion_
- _more/fewer + countable noun; more/less + uncountable noun_

### Task 1 accuracy traps

- Incorrect: _The figure was two times higher than 10._
- Clear: _The figure was twice as high as 10._ / _The figure was double the 2010 level._
- Incorrect: _The number of cars was higher than buses._
- Correct: _The number of cars was higher than the number of buses._
- Better: _There were more cars than buses._

### Practice 13

Correct the comparison.

1. The unemployment rate was more low in 2020.
2. There was less students in private schools.
3. Car ownership was twice higher than in 2000.
4. The figure for France was higher than Germany.

## Unit 14: Passive voice

Use the passive when the action or result matters more than the actor.

- Active: _The government introduced the scheme in 2021._
- Passive: _The scheme was introduced in 2021._

**Form:** _be + past participle_

- present: _is produced_
- past: _was produced_
- present perfect: _has been produced_
- modal: _can be reduced_

### IELTS uses

- Processes: _The beans are dried before they are packaged._
- Formal reporting: _It is widely argued that…_
- Unknown actor: _Several errors were identified._

Do not hide responsibility unnecessarily.

- Weaker: _Mistakes were made._
- Clearer: _The company made serious safety errors._

### Practice 14

Rewrite in the passive.

1. The council built the bridge in 2019.
2. Manufacturers can recycle the material.
3. Researchers have linked air pollution to respiratory illness.
4. The factory packages the final product by hand.

## Unit 15: Reported views and reporting verbs

Use reporting structures to present claims precisely.

- _Researchers argue that early education reduces inequality._
- _The report suggests that the policy has had a modest effect._
- _Critics claim that the scheme is too expensive._
- _The evidence indicates that demand is stabilising._

### Verb patterns

- _argue/claim/suggest that + clause_
- _encourage/allow/require + object + to-infinitive_
- _prevent + object + from + -ing_
- _attribute + noun + to + noun/-ing_

Avoid empty attribution such as _Some people say…_ when no group or evidence is identified. In IELTS Task 2, the writer may state a reasoned position directly.

### Practice 15

Correct the structure.

1. The report suggests the government to increase taxes.
2. The law prevents companies to collect data without consent.
3. Researchers attribute the decline from stricter regulation.
4. The policy allows residents recycling household waste.

## Unit 16: Participle clauses and concise academic style

Participle clauses can reduce repetition when the subject is the same.

- _Because it was designed for low-income families, the scheme was heavily subsidised._
- _Designed for low-income families, the scheme was heavily subsidised._
- _After analysing the data, researchers identified three trends._

Avoid **dangling modifiers**.

- Incorrect: _After analysing the data, three trends were identified._ Who analysed it?
- Correct: _After analysing the data, the researchers identified three trends._

Use these structures selectively. Accuracy is more valuable than density.

### Practice 16

Correct the dangling modifier.

1. By investing in railways, congestion can be reduced by governments.
2. After reviewing the results, several errors were obvious.
3. Having completed the survey, the responses were entered into a database.

## Unit 17: Hedging and precision

Academic arguments often require qualified rather than absolute claims.

| Too absolute                         | More defensible                                                              |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _Social media causes depression._    | _Heavy social-media use may contribute to poor mental health in some users._ |
| _All students learn better online._  | _Some students may benefit from the flexibility of online learning._         |
| _This policy will solve congestion._ | _This policy could help reduce congestion if it is properly enforced._       |

Useful hedges: _may, might, could, tends to, appears to, is likely to, in many cases, to some extent, generally, relatively._

Hedging does not mean uncertainty everywhere. State well-supported facts directly.

# Part IV — Punctuation that affects accuracy

## Unit 18: Full stops, commas, semicolons and apostrophes

### Full stop

Use it to end a complete sentence. If a sentence contains several major ideas, divide it.

### Comma

Use commas:

- after an opening dependent clause: _Although costs rose, demand remained stable._
- around non-defining information: _The policy, which began in May, was unpopular._
- before a coordinating conjunction joining two complete clauses: _Costs rose, but demand remained stable._
- in a list: _The scheme is efficient, affordable and reliable._

Do not place a comma between a subject and its verb.

- Incorrect: _The rapid growth of cities, creates housing pressure._

### Semicolon

Join closely related complete sentences.

- _The scheme reduced congestion; however, it was expensive to administer._

**However** cannot usually join two sentences with commas alone.

- Incorrect: _The scheme was costly, however, it was effective._
- Correct: _The scheme was costly. However, it was effective._
- Correct: _The scheme was costly; however, it was effective._

### Apostrophe

- possession: _the government’s policy; students’ needs_
- contraction: _it’s = it is; don’t = do not_
- no apostrophe in plural nouns: _policies, 1990s, students_
- **its** = possessive; **it’s** = it is

### Practice 18

Correct the punctuation.

1. Although the policy was unpopular it reduced waste.
2. The city expanded rapidly, however housing remained limited.
3. The governments decision affected many company’s.
4. Public transport which is heavily subsidised remains affordable.
5. The rising cost of food, affects low-income households.

# Part V — Grammar by IELTS task

## Writing Task 1 Academic: highest-value grammar

1. **Tense based on dates:** past for completed periods, present perfect for change continuing to now, future or projection language for forecasts.
2. **Comparison:** _higher than, the largest proportion, twice as high as, respectively_.
3. **Change structures:** _rose from X to Y; increased by 20%; remained stable at 40%_.
4. **Complex comparison:** _While car ownership increased, bicycle use declined._
5. **Passive voice for processes:** _The material is heated and then transferred to a storage tank._
6. **Approximation:** _approximately, just over, slightly below, nearly_.

### Model paragraph

_In 2000, approximately 35% of commuters travelled by car, compared with 25% who used buses. Over the following decade, the proportion driving rose steadily to 50%, whereas bus use declined to just under 20%. By contrast, rail travel, which initially accounted for the smallest share, nearly doubled._

**Grammar map:** past simple; comparison with _compared with_; relative clause; _whereas_ contrast; precise adverbs.

## Writing Task 2: highest-value grammar

A strong essay normally contains:

- accurate simple sentences for key claims;
- complex sentences for reason, contrast and condition;
- relative clauses for definition or detail;
- modals and hedging for measured claims;
- parallel structures for clear lists;
- controlled referencing to avoid repetition.

### Model paragraph

_Governments should subsidise public transport because affordable fares can persuade commuters to leave their cars at home. If services were frequent and reliable, more residents would regard buses and trains as realistic alternatives. This shift, which would reduce both congestion and emissions, could also improve access to employment for low-income workers. Although such investment is expensive, its long-term social and environmental benefits are likely to outweigh the initial cost._

**Grammar map:** reason clause; second conditional; non-defining relative clause; modal verbs; paired nouns; concession clause; possessive determiner.

## General Training Task 1: tone and grammar

Match grammar to purpose and relationship.

- Formal request: _I would be grateful if you could provide a replacement._
- Complaint: _I am writing to express my concern about…_
- Apology: _I sincerely apologise for the inconvenience caused._
- Informal invitation: _Would you like to stay with us next weekend?_

Avoid contractions in highly formal letters. Contractions are natural in informal letters.

## Speaking: flexible but natural grammar

Speaking is not a written essay. Use contractions and natural clause patterns.

- Part 1: _I’ve lived here for about five years, and what I like most is the neighbourhood atmosphere._
- Part 2: _The person I’d like to talk about is a teacher who encouraged me to become more independent._
- Part 3: _Although online education is more accessible, I don’t think it can completely replace face-to-face learning._

### Speaking range toolkit

- present perfect for experience: _I’ve visited…_
- past tenses for stories: _I was travelling when…_
- conditionals for speculation: _If cities had more parks, residents would…_
- relative clauses for description: _a place where…_
- comparatives for analysis: _far more convenient than…_
- modals for cautious opinions: _It might lead to…_

Do not memorise long “Band 9” sentences. Examiners assess average performance across the whole Speaking test, and memorised language often sounds inflexible.

## Reading and Listening: grammar as an answer-finding tool

Before a gap, predict the required form.

- _a/an + \_\_\__ → likely singular countable noun or adjective + noun
- _many/several + \_\_\__ → plural countable noun
- _much/less + \_\_\__ → uncountable noun
- _to + \_\_\__ → base verb
- _preposition + \_\_\__ → noun or -ing form
- _has/have + \_\_\__ → past participle
- _is/are + \_\_\__ → adjective, noun, -ing form or past participle depending on meaning

Always obey the word limit and copy spelling accurately.

# Part VI — Common error clinic

| Incorrect                        | Correct                                       | Reason                                           |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| _People is…_                     | _People are…_                                 | _People_ is plural.                              |
| _The number of cars have risen._ | _The number of cars has risen._               | The head subject is _number_.                    |
| _Many research shows…_           | _Much research shows…_ / _Many studies show…_ | _Research_ is uncountable.                       |
| _An information_                 | _Some information_                            | _Information_ is uncountable.                    |
| _Discuss about the issue_        | _Discuss the issue_                           | _Discuss_ takes no _about_.                      |
| _Focus in improving_             | _Focus on improving_                          | Dependent preposition + -ing.                    |
| _Although…, but…_                | _Although…, …_ / _…, but…_                    | Do not use both conjunctions.                    |
| _If it will improve…_            | _If it improves…_                             | First conditional uses present in the if-clause. |
| _Must to act_                    | _Must act_                                    | Modal + base verb.                               |
| _Since five years_               | _For five years_                              | _For_ expresses duration.                        |
| _More easier_                    | _Easier_                                      | Do not use double comparison.                    |
| _Less cars_                      | _Fewer cars_                                  | Use _fewer_ with countable plurals.              |
| _Students which…_                | _Students who…_                               | Use _who_ for people.                            |
| _The reason is because…_         | _The reason is that…_ / _This is because…_    | Avoid redundant structure.                       |
| _In 2010, sales have fallen._    | _In 2010, sales fell._                        | Finished past time requires past simple.         |

## Practice D: Task 2 paragraph upgrade

Rewrite this paragraph with accurate grammar and greater variety:

_Cars cause pollution. Many people use cars. Public transport is not reliable. Government should improve it. People will use buses. Traffic will reduce. It is expensive. It is necessary._

### Model upgrade

_Although private cars are a major source of urban pollution, many commuters rely on them because public transport is often unreliable. If governments improved the frequency and coverage of bus services, more people would be willing to leave their cars at home, which could reduce both traffic and emissions. Such investment may be expensive; nevertheless, it is necessary for the long-term health and efficiency of cities._

## Practice E: Speaking prompts

Answer each for 45–60 seconds and use the target structure naturally.

1. **Present perfect:** How has your hometown changed in recent years?
2. **Past narrative:** Describe a time when you learned something difficult.
3. **Relative clause:** Describe a person who influenced you.
4. **Second conditional:** What would improve life in large cities?
5. **Concession:** Do the advantages of social media outweigh the disadvantages?
6. **Speculation:** How might education change in the next 20 years?

Record each answer. Transcribe one minute, underline every verb, circle each complex structure, and correct repeated errors.
