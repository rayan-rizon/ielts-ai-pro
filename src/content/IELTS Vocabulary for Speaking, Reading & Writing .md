# IELTS Vocabulary for Speaking, Reading & Writing — Bands 7, 8 & 9

<aside>
🎯

**Purpose.** This bank separates vocabulary by IELTS skill and target band. Learn complete phrases and collocations, then practise them in context. Accuracy and flexibility matter more than using unnecessarily rare words.

</aside>

---

## Part I — Speaking vocabulary

### A. Giving and developing opinions

| Word / phrase                 | Function                       | Natural example                                                    |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| from my perspective           | state a personal view          | From my perspective, public transport should be subsidised.        |
| I am inclined to believe      | express a considered opinion   | I am inclined to believe that remote work will remain common.      |
| as far as I am concerned      | give a firm personal view      | As far as I am concerned, practical skills deserve more attention. |
| I would argue that            | present a defensible claim     | I would argue that prevention is cheaper than treatment.           |
| there is a strong case for    | support a proposal             | There is a strong case for investing in renewable energy.          |
| to my mind                    | introduce an opinion naturally | To my mind, the benefits outweigh the drawbacks.                   |
| I remain unconvinced that     | express polite disagreement    | I remain unconvinced that longer working hours raise productivity. |
| it seems reasonable to assume | make a cautious inference      | It seems reasonable to assume that demand will increase.           |
| what matters most is          | identify the priority          | What matters most is equal access to education.                    |
| the underlying issue is       | move to the root cause         | The underlying issue is a shortage of affordable housing.          |

### B. Agreeing, disagreeing and balancing

| Word / phrase                    | Function                  | Natural example                                                        |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I completely agree               | strong agreement          | I completely agree that children need outdoor play.                    |
| I agree to some extent           | partial agreement         | I agree to some extent, although cost is still a barrier.              |
| that is a valid point            | acknowledge another view  | That is a valid point, but enforcement also matters.                   |
| I see where that view comes from | show understanding        | I see where that view comes from, yet the evidence is mixed.           |
| I would take a different view    | disagree politely         | I would take a different view because the policy may widen inequality. |
| that does not necessarily follow | challenge logic           | Prices rose, but it does not necessarily follow that demand increased. |
| the opposite may be true         | introduce a counterclaim  | In rural areas, the opposite may be true.                              |
| on the one hand … on the other   | balance two sides         | On the one hand it saves time; on the other, it reduces interaction.   |
| there is some truth in that      | concede before qualifying | There is some truth in that, but it ignores long-term costs.           |
| it depends largely on            | avoid overgeneralisation  | It depends largely on a person’s circumstances.                        |

### C. Fluency, explanation and examples

| Word / phrase                   | Function                      | Natural example                                                     |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| in other words                  | restate an idea               | In other words, the scheme is not financially viable.               |
| what I mean is                  | clarify speech                | What I mean is that convenience can encourage waste.                |
| to put it another way           | paraphrase                    | To put it another way, technology is a tool rather than a solution. |
| a good example would be         | introduce evidence            | A good example would be bicycle-sharing schemes.                    |
| take … for instance             | give a compact example        | Take Singapore, for instance.                                       |
| this is particularly evident in | identify a clear case         | This is particularly evident in large cities.                       |
| the main reason is that         | explain a cause               | The main reason is that rent has risen faster than wages.           |
| this can be attributed to       | give a formal cause           | This can be attributed to improved healthcare.                      |
| as a consequence                | show a result                 | As a consequence, fewer people commute daily.                       |
| broadly speaking                | make a careful generalisation | Broadly speaking, younger people adapt faster to new platforms.     |

| resilient                    | able to recover from difficulty | a resilient community                         |
| approachable                 | friendly and easy to talk to    | an approachable teacher                       |

## Part II — Reading vocabulary

### A. Argument and evidence

| Word / phrase | Meaning                            | Common pattern                |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| assert        | state confidently                  | the author asserts that…      |
| contend       | argue or maintain                  | researchers contend that…     |
| claim         | state something as true            | the report claims that…       |
| imply         | suggest without stating directly   | the passage implies that…     |
| infer         | reach a conclusion from evidence   | readers can infer that…       |
| substantiate  | support with evidence              | substantiate a claim          |
| refute        | prove an argument wrong            | refute the hypothesis         |
| corroborate   | confirm with supporting evidence   | corroborate the findings      |
| acknowledge   | recognise a fact or limitation     | acknowledge the risks         |
| challenge     | question an accepted idea          | challenge conventional wisdom |
| premise       | an idea on which an argument rests | a flawed premise              |
| rationale     | the reason behind a decision       | the rationale for the policy  |

| ------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |

## Part III — Writing vocabulary

### A. Introducing and framing an argument

| Word / phrase                             | Function                    | Model use                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| it is often argued that                   | introduce a common position | It is often argued that university should be free.                   |
| a central issue is whether                | define the debate           | A central issue is whether governments should regulate social media. |
| this essay contends that                  | state a clear thesis        | This essay contends that prevention should take priority.            |
| there is growing concern that             | introduce a problem         | There is growing concern that automation may displace workers.       |
| the question is not merely … but          | add complexity              | The question is not merely cost but long-term value.                 |
| proponents maintain that                  | report the supporting side  | Proponents maintain that the measure will reduce congestion.         |
| opponents point out that                  | report the opposing side    | Opponents point out that rural areas lack alternatives.              |
| the debate centres on                     | identify the core dispute   | The debate centres on personal freedom and public safety.            |
| a distinction must be drawn between       | separate concepts           | A distinction must be drawn between access and effective use.        |
| this issue should be assessed in terms of | set evaluation criteria     | The policy should be assessed in terms of fairness and feasibility.  |

### B. Analysis, cause and consequence

| Word / phrase             | Meaning                        | Natural collocation                         |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| attributable to           | caused by                      | the decline is attributable to rising costs |
| a contributing factor     | one of several causes          | a major contributing factor                 |
| underlying cause          | fundamental reason             | address the underlying causes               |
| far-reaching consequences | broad, important effects       | have far-reaching consequences              |
| unintended consequence    | an unplanned result            | create unintended consequences              |
| compound the problem      | make a problem worse           | high interest rates compound the problem    |
| place a strain on         | create pressure                | place a strain on public services           |
| pose a threat to          | create danger                  | pose a threat to biodiversity               |
| yield benefits            | produce advantages             | yield long-term benefits                    |
| act as a catalyst for     | accelerate change              | act as a catalyst for innovation            |
| reinforce                 | strengthen an effect or belief | reinforce social inequality                 |
| offset                    | counterbalance                 | offset implementation costs                 |

### C. Evaluation and recommendations

| Word / phrase     | Meaning                        | Natural collocation             |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| viable            | capable of succeeding          | a financially viable solution   |
| sustainable       | maintainable over time         | a sustainable approach          |
| equitable         | fair to different groups       | equitable access                |
| cost-effective    | good value for cost            | a cost-effective intervention   |
| counterproductive | producing the opposite result  | a counterproductive restriction |
| disproportionate  | too large or small in relation | a disproportionate burden       |
| feasible          | practical and possible         | a feasible alternative          |
| indispensable     | absolutely necessary           | an indispensable public service |
| warrant           | justify or deserve             | warrant further investment      |
| prioritise        | treat as most important        | prioritise preventive care      |
| implement         | put into operation             | implement stricter standards    |

### D. Cohesion and precise qualification

| Word / phrase     | Function                             | Model use                                                    |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| nevertheless      | contrast despite the previous point  | The plan is costly; nevertheless, it may save money later.   |
| conversely        | introduce an opposite situation      | Urban demand rose; conversely, rural demand declined.        |
| consequently      | show a result                        | Fuel prices increased; consequently, commuting fell.         |
| by contrast       | compare a clear difference           | By contrast, the second group remained stable.               |
| in this respect   | refer to a specific aspect           | In this respect, local authorities are better placed to act. |
| to a large extent | qualify the degree                   | Success depends, to a large extent, on enforcement.          |
| albeit            | express a concise concession         | The reform was effective, albeit expensive.                  |
| notwithstanding   | show that something remains true     | Notwithstanding these concerns, the policy has merit.        |
| in the absence of | state what happens without something | In the absence of regulation, abuse may continue.            |
| provided that     | state a condition                    | The scheme can work provided that funding is stable.         |
| in the short term | specify immediate effects            | In the short term, prices may rise.                          |
| in the long run   | specify eventual effects             | In the long run, efficiency should improve.                  |

## Band progression: one idea at three levels

| Basic idea                         | Band 7 expression                                   | Band 8 expression                                                      | Band 9 expression                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The policy is bad for poor people. | The policy is detrimental to low-income households. | The policy places a disproportionate burden on low-income households.  | The policy may inadvertently perpetuate entrenched socioeconomic inequality.                                |
| Technology helps communication.    | Technology facilitates communication.               | Digital platforms facilitate rapid knowledge sharing.                  | Digital platforms can engender unprecedented connectivity, albeit with implications for privacy.            |
| The problem has many causes.       | Several factors contribute to the problem.          | The problem stems from multiple interrelated factors.                  | This is a multifaceted issue rooted in systemic and deeply entrenched conditions.                           |
| The evidence is not clear.         | The evidence is limited.                            | The available evidence is inconclusive.                                | The evidence remains equivocal and does not substantiate an unequivocal conclusion.                         |
| Governments should act.            | Governments should implement effective measures.    | Policymakers should prioritise targeted, evidence-based interventions. | Policymakers must reconcile competing priorities and implement proportionate, empirically grounded reforms. |
