# Global SaaS/AI subscription refund policy: standards cross-check

**Research date:** 22 September 2026
**Scope:** Global/Stripe-facing English refund and cancellation policy for an online SaaS product with AI-credit usage. Bangladesh-specific payment rules and pages are out of scope.
**Caveat:** This is product/compliance research, not legal advice. Applicable rules depend on customer location, consumer status, contract structure, how the service is supplied, and the checkout disclosures/consents. Have counsel review the final policy and checkout flow for the markets served.

## Findings

### Stripe: make the policy findable and retain proof

I did not find a universal Stripe-mandated refund window for SaaS in the public guidance reviewed. Stripe's account-risk guidance says to make refund terms clear and easy to find, provide a straightforward support route, and keep records; it also says an accessible refund policy can help prevent disputes. Stripe's dispute evidence model provides fields for the refund policy and subscription-cancellation policy shown to the customer, evidence of how/when the refund terms were disclosed before purchase, access/activity logs for digital products, and an explanation supporting a refund refusal. This supports having a distinct, public refund/cancellation page and retaining the exact policy version and acceptance/disclosure evidence. These are Stripe risk/evidence practices, not a substitute for consumer law or a guarantee of account approval.

Sources: [Stripe account-review guidance](https://support.stripe.com/express/questions/requests-for-additional-business-information), [Stripe dispute evidence fields](https://docs.stripe.com/api/disputes/update).

### Consumer-law constraints on “non-refundable after AI-credit use”

- **EU:** For distance contracts, the Consumer Rights Directive generally gives consumers 14 days to withdraw. If a paid service starts during that period at the consumer's express request, a proportionate charge for what has been supplied may be due when the consumer withdraws; if the information/request requirements are not met, the consumer may owe nothing for that early supply. A service withdrawal right is lost after full performance only where the required prior express consent/request and acknowledgement are obtained. For non-tangible digital content, the exception requires prior express consent to begin, acknowledgement of loss of the withdrawal right, and confirmation of the contract. Therefore, do not assume that spending AI credits automatically extinguishes every customer's statutory withdrawal right: a subscription/SaaS service, discrete digital content, the consent flow, and whether the contract is fully performed can matter. Separately, EU digital-content/service conformity rules give remedies, including price reduction or termination in qualifying cases; a blanket commercial “no refunds” term must not purport to remove mandatory statutory rights.

  Sources: [EU Consumer Rights Directive, Articles 6–9, 14 and 16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02011L0083-20220528), [EU Digital Content and Digital Services Directive 2019/770, Articles 13–16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/770/oj/eng).

- **UK:** The Consumer Contracts Regulations likewise distinguish services from digital content. For a service begun during the 14-day cancellation period, the consumer must have expressly requested early supply; on cancellation, a proportionate amount may be payable if required disclosures were made. The cancellation right ends after full service performance only with the prescribed acknowledgement. Digital content has a separate express-consent/acknowledgement route. The Consumer Rights Act also provides non-excludable quality/description remedies for paid digital content. In addition, the UK Government's 2026 implementation response says the new subscription-contract regime is planned for Spring 2027, so it should not be described as already in force as of this research date.

  Sources: [Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, regulations 29–37](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/contents), [Consumer Rights Act 2015, including sections 34–47](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/contents), [UK Government 2026 implementation response](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/consultation-on-the-implementation-of-the-new-subscription-contracts-regime/outcome/government-response-to-consultation-on-the-implementation-of-the-new-subscription-contracts-regime-web-accessible-version).

- **United States:** ROSCA applies to online negative-option subscriptions and requires clear/conspicuous material terms before billing information is obtained, express informed consent before charging, and a simple way to stop recurring charges. ROSCA is not itself a general refund/cooling-off entitlement. The FTC Cooling-Off Rule is for covered sales at a home or certain other locations, not a universal online-SaaS refund window. State laws and other federal protections can add obligations; do not present this summary as exhaustive. The FTC's 2024 Click-to-Cancel amendments were vacated by the Eighth Circuit on 8 July 2025; the FTC's current rule page shows renewed rulemaking activity in 2026, so avoid describing those vacated amendments as currently binding.

  Sources: [U.S. Code, 15 U.S.C. § 8403 (ROSCA)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title15-section8403), [FTC Cooling-Off Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/cooling-period-sales-made-home-or-other-locations), [FTC Negative Option Rule page/current rulemaking](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/negative-option-rule), [Eighth Circuit vacatur opinion](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/25/07/243137P.pdf), [FTC's March 2026 proposed-rulemaking notice](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/p064202negativeoptionruleanprm.pdf).

## Policy design implications

1. Keep the global refund page and Bangladesh refund page distinct: different payment rails and legal regimes should not share mixed or conflicting copy. The global page should not refer to bKash/Nagad or Bangladesh-specific procedures.
2. Separate **cancelling renewal** from **requesting a refund**. Explain when cancellation takes effect, whether access continues through the paid-through date, how to cancel, which renewal charges are refundable (if any), how to request a refund, processing method/time, and support contact. State any 7-day voluntary refund offer's start/end point, eligibility, covered purchase (for example initial subscription only), and credit-use effect before checkout. Treat a 7-day offer as an extra commercial promise, not as a replacement for a longer mandatory withdrawal right that applies in a customer's jurisdiction. In particular, it cannot shorten an EU/UK 14-day statutory withdrawal period where the relevant consumer right applies.
3. For paid AI credits, a disclosed rule may make consumed credits or meaningful paid-feature use ineligible for a _voluntary_ money-back guarantee, and can make unused credits non-refundable/expire on the stated date where lawful. Define exactly what “used” means and preserve whatever remedies applicable law requires, including qualifying withdrawal or digital-service conformity remedies; handle unauthorized/duplicate payments under applicable law and payment rules. Do not claim that all credits being used automatically waives statutory rights everywhere.
4. Show the material renewal, charge, cancellation, refund, and AI-credit-use terms at checkout next to the purchase action—not only behind a footer link. Use clear affirmative consent where required, and retain timestamped evidence of the exact policy/checkout text accepted, billing events, cancellation request/effective date, service access, and AI-credit use. Make cancellation straightforward and never keep charging after a valid cancellation takes effect.
5. State that mandatory consumer rights prevail over the voluntary policy. Avoid an unqualified “no refunds under any circumstances” statement. Have counsel determine whether this service's AI outputs/credits are service units or digital content in each target market, and design any early-performance consent/acknowledgement flow accordingly.
